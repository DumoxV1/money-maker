# Business Kit: AI White-Label Inbound Voice-Agent Ops

## Problem
Small web, marketing, and automation agencies can resell AI phone agents, but the invisible fulfillment work is substantial: provisioning numbers, grounding each agent in approved facts, configuring routing and scheduling, testing edge cases, reviewing transcripts, and keeping the knowledge base current. Generic voice-agent vendors provide infrastructure, not a dependable white-label operations desk.

## Audience
Small agencies that want to resell inbound lead-intake and appointment agents to service businesses without hiring a conversational-AI specialist or having staff listen to every call.

## Offer
An agency-branded, inbound-only voice-agent operations service. Approved business FAQs and policies go in; a configured phone line, cited knowledge base, routing and calendar workflow, synthetic call-test report, ongoing transcript QA, bounded knowledge-base updates, uptime alerts, and a monthly white-label report come out. The agency owns the client relationship and vendor accounts; the AI ops desk remains invisible.

## Pricing
Charge €249-599 for setup and €99-299/month per managed line, with telephony and model usage passed through transparently from the agency's vendor account. Include one narrow test line or synthetic-call audit as the proof offer; price regulated, multilingual, outbound, or custom-integration work separately rather than silently expanding scope.

## 7-Day Launch Plan
- **Day 1** — Fix the safe beachhead: inbound FAQ, lead capture, and appointment requests for non-regulated service businesses. Explicitly exclude emergency response, medical/legal/financial advice, debt collection, payments, and outbound dialing.
- **Day 2** — Define a machine-readable intake schema for approved facts, opening hours, service area, booking rules, escalation destinations, prohibited answers, retention choices, and required disclosures.
- **Day 3** — Build a vendor-neutral provisioning workflow that creates the number, agent, cited knowledge base, calendar/message action, spend cap, fallback path, and versioned configuration through supported APIs.
- **Day 4** — Create a synthetic caller suite covering normal requests, accents and interruptions, unknown questions, prompt-injection attempts, personal-data requests, after-hours messages, and emergency language. Fail closed and route or take a message when confidence is low.
- **Day 5** — Add transcript redaction, factuality and task-completion scoring, latency and failure alerts, bounded patch proposals, rollback, and an agency-branded QA report. Test only on an owned sandbox number.
- **Day 6** — Publish an anonymized test report, scope and data-handling notice; wire checkout, agency-owned credential connection, disclosure approval, deployment approval, revocation, and deletion flows.
- **Day 7** — Identify agencies with a public voice/chatbot offer, send a small number of compliant personalized audit invitations, and let qualified prospects provision a sandbox through self-serve onboarding.

## AI Execution
Once an agency connects its own least-privilege telephony and voice-platform credentials and submits client-approved source material, a scheduled AI agent validates the scope, builds a source-cited knowledge base, provisions an inbound number and workflow by API, connects calendar or structured message intake, and runs synthetic calls against a machine-readable acceptance suite. It scores transcripts for factual grounding, disclosure, task completion, latency, routing, privacy, and prohibited behavior; failed configurations are rolled back or patched within approved bounds and retested before deployment. In production, webhook events trigger automated redaction, sampling, QA, alerts, knowledge-drift checks, report generation, billing, and scoped support. Material policy changes require client approval, while routine source-synchronized updates can deploy automatically with versioning and rollback. The side-hustle operator never joins a call, listens manually, visits a site, or performs fulfillment.

## Legal and Quality Guardrails
- Keep the standard offer inbound-only. Do not add outbound calling unless the agency separately proves jurisdiction-specific consent, identification, calling-hour, suppression-list, and telemarketing compliance.
- Open every call with an accurate automated-assistant disclosure. Enable recording or transcription only after the client configures legally sufficient notice/consent for every served jurisdiction.
- Use stock synthetic voices with commercial rights. Never clone a person's voice or imply a human identity without that person's documented authorization.
- Exclude emergency dispatch and high-impact medical, legal, financial, employment, housing, insurance, debt, and eligibility decisions. Emergency language triggers a fixed safety message and approved routing, never an improvised answer.
- Ground answers only in versioned, client-approved sources. Unknown or conflicting facts trigger message capture or routing; the agent must not invent prices, availability, guarantees, credentials, or policies.
- Minimize collected data, redact sensitive transcript fields, encrypt credentials, set short configurable retention, honor deletion requests, and never collect card details or authentication secrets by voice.
- Use least-privilege client-owned vendor accounts, spending and duration caps, signed webhooks, secret rotation, audit logs, rollback, uptime monitoring, and a tested kill switch.
- Respect vendor terms, number-registration requirements, anti-spam rules, community rules, rate limits, and every outreach opt-out.
